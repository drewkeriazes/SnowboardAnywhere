# Developer guide

The detail behind the top-level README: what is in the game, where the code lives, every tool,
URL parameters and the milestone history. The pipeline that builds resort packs is in
[MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere](https://github.com/drewkeriazes/MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere) (EPIC-16). Process docs: [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md)
(current state), [BOARD.md](BOARD.md) (kanban), [workspace-and-sessions.md](workspace-and-sessions.md)
(where things live, multi-session rules). Plan of record: [bonvo-teardown-and-build-plan.md](bonvo-teardown-and-build-plan.md).

## Features in detail

- Terrain: quadtree LOD over the 262 km far ring, 1 m near the rider, snow / rock / scree / groomed material; forested ground darkens from the tree impostor ring outward so the far forest reads dark. Distant forest (FEAT-153, `pack/CoverSet.ts`): past about 300 m the shader takes the forest share per pixel from the pack's cover levels (`cover/*.png`, a mipmapped texture per level, up to 3), so the real forest edges and the ski trails cut through it show at any distance; to 1.2 km it reads the level's closed stands of drawn trees (G of the texture, the `stands` image) and from 2.8 km the forest as a mass (R), so open slopes with a few trees stay white up close and far timber stays solid (BUG-069); on a pack with stands it keeps G as far as the generated stems are drawn (`uStemReach`, 2.5 km at full density) and takes R by 1.5 times that, so sparse stands stay their trees on white snow while those are drawn (BUG-120); chunks coarser than 8 m take forest, rock and canopy height from the same levels per vertex instead of the procedural treeline (`proceduralForest`, now only where no level reaches or before they load; on a pack with `treeline: null`, FEAT-234, it opens by slope and noise patches only, with no treeline or open valley floor). From the impostors' reach (`uLiftNear`, Vegetation's FAR_RADIUS: 4.5 km at full tree density, nearer on lower density settings) to 2 km past it, chunks coarser than 8 m rise by canopy height x cover x 0.7 (the `lift` attribute, applied in the vertex shader) so forested ridges read as treed; the far timber has a crown-scale texture that fades to an even tone once a pixel is wider than a crown. Forest from high up (FEAT-154, `docs/research/far-terrain.md` section 7): how dark forested ground goes is a gap fraction, `1 - ((1 - s) / (1 - rho s))^((1 + 2.5 sqrt(1 - mu^2)) / mu)` of the cover share `s` and the cosine `mu` between the ground normal and the view (the 2.5, `TREE_ASPECT`, is a conifer's side silhouette over its plan area: tall trees close the gaps sooner in a slanted view, BUG-069), so grazing views from the snow see a closed mass and views from the air see the openings; inside the trees' reach `rho` (`uForestDrawn`, 0.7) is the part of the canopy the drawn trees already cover, and outside the pack's tree instances (`uTreeRect`, set by `Terrain` from `meta.instances`) the ground carries the forest from the first metre. Sunlit canopy takes `uCanopySun` (0.22) of the direct light and a forested pixel is `uTimberMix` (0.97) timber. The ridges round the resort (BUG-081): the pack's trees and the far tree ring's (`farTrees/`, loaded with the same cells, `treeRing` cells past the grid) draw as impostors out to 9 km at full tree density (`FAR_RADIUS`, twice the thinning start). Past 4.5 km (`THIN_RADIUS`) an impostor stays with chance (4.5 km / d)^2 and grows by d / 4.5 km (`THIN_GLSL` in `world/trees/materials.ts`), so the ground under crowns holds while the count falls; `Vegetation.packFar` leaves the ones that can't show out of the buffers (the draw is per tree, packed into `iSpec`'s fraction). Past the generated stems' reach (`vegetationStemReach`, near radius + 1 km) the impostors grow by `STEM_GROW` (2.2, the square root of the stems' 4.2x extra canopy on Vail) to stand in for the stems, and the terrain lets them carry 0.92 of the forest (`FOREST_DRAWN_FAR`, `uStemReach`) instead of laying flat timber between them; both only on packs with stands. The Low preset keeps one 600 m reach. On the Low preset with the clump impostors (FEAT-292, the Far forest bullet below; every pack with cover levels; Medium and High keep everything above, BUG-130) the per-tree impostors stop at the preset's 600 m, so the thinning, `STEM_GROW` and BUG-121's tail past the far edge never show: the trees end at the handover (`vegetationTreeEnd`, the shade map's shadows with them), `vegetationFarRadius` is the clumps' far edge (9 km), the canopy lift starts there, and in the clump band the ground lays only the timber the clumps miss (`uClump`, `CLUMP_RHO` 0.7 as `rho`). `?noclumps` gives the far forest described here. The impostors run on, still thinning, 700 m past the reach (`TREE_TAIL`; about where the loaded cells used to end on average) and end over the last fifth of that (`farFadeWidth` in `world/trees/index.ts`, the `uFarEdge` uniform): the chance falls on to none there, tree by tree, so they end at a set distance; tree cells load 250 m further out (`STREAM_AHEAD`), so a cell's trees and stems are in before any of them can show, and the shade map's shadows of the trees past the end go with them (`uTreeEnd` in the terrain shader). BUG-121: they used to run on to the edge of whichever 1 km cells were loaded, and on Low whole rows of trees appeared on the skyline as a cell arrived. Open ground far off (BUG-081): from 900 m (full by 2.2 km), ungroomed snow where the season's snowpack is thinner than 0.25 to 1 m shows the sage, grass and low shrub poking through it, up to 0.6 of a pixel in 40 m and 12 m patches, so low sun-facing slopes read darker than the north-facing resort side. The depth is FEAT-159's (`sim/Snowpack.ts`: month, elevation, aspect, new snow; `SNOWPACK_GLSL` is the same formula for the shader, set through `Terrain.setSnowpack` next to the shrubs' state). Tree shadows past the shadow map (FEAT-168, FEAT-171): the sun's shadow camera reaches about 260 m around the rider and only the LOD0 / LOD1 trees (to 400 m) cast into it, so past that the terrain shader takes the direct sun off the snow itself (the blue sky fill stays, so the shade is blue). Near and mid distance it reads the shade map (`world/TreeShade.ts`, owned by `Vegetation`): every drawn tree's shadow as one quad with a cone's outline (the crown-base disc joined to the tip's shadow, from the tree's height, crown radius and species: `crownBase`, and bare aspen, bare hardwood and snags let more sun through), laid from the trunk away from the sun and stretched by the ground slope under the tree (from a 20 m height grid per cell, `Pack.demHeight`), drawn from above into a three-layer array target around the camera (half-widths 512, 1024 and 2048 m at 2048 px, so 0.5, 1 and 2 m texels; 1024 px with `?lowgfx`). A layer is redrawn, at most one per frame, when the camera has moved a fifth of its half-width, when the sun has turned 0.25 degrees, or when a cell of trees arrives or leaves; the terrain picks the layer whose texel is about a pixel on the ground. Where the shade map doesn't reach, `treeShadow` steps 8 times toward the sun through the pack cover level's map of where trees stand (B of that level's texture, `uTreeMap`; from the `stands` image's G, 4 m cells), over `TREE_SHADOW_H` (12 m) / tan(sun elevation), capped at 48 m, but only where one of its cells is under about 2 to 3 pixels (about 1.5 km and out at 1080p), so it never paints soft patches nearer. The handover to the shadow map (`Terrain.setTreeShadeGate`, set per frame in `RideFrame`) is at the LOD1 edge and outside the sun's shadow camera (its shadow matrix), from the camera distance only with cascades on, and from 200 m (full by 320 m) with shadows off. None in flat light or on the canopy. `uDebugFar` switches terms off for A/B stills (x forest, y rock and talus, z snow drift normals; w paints the forest darkening red), for example `SHOT_EVAL` or a `tools/aerial.mjs` variant setting `__sim.terrain.material.userData.uniforms.uDebugFar.value.set(1, 0, 0, 0)`.
- Trees (FEAT-156, `world/trees/`, drawn by `world/Vegetation.ts`): a procedural asset kit, nothing downloaded. `species.ts` is the table of the ten species (ids as in the pack, DATA-020: 0 Engelmann spruce, 1 aspen, 2 krummholz, 3 subalpine fir, 4 lodgepole, 5 Douglas-fir, 6 limber pine, 7 snag; FEAT-233, `treeSpecies` 3 packs: 8 bare hardwood, the eastern oak, beech and maple in winter, a grey-brown twig crown on limbs that hold snow at the forks, and 9 eastern hemlock, darker and softer than spruce with drooping sprays and a nodding leader) with their shape, colours, whorls and variant weights; packs without `meta.treeSpecies` (0 to 2 only) split their conifers 60/40 into spruce and fir by a position hash, and an id past the table draws as spruce. `textures.ts` draws one 1024 x 1024 atlas at load (12 grey branch-spray cells and 8 bark swatches, tinted per species by vertex colour; transparent texels carry the cell's mean colour and each mip keeps the cell's alpha coverage). `generate.ts` builds every species x 4 variants (seeded: tier count, gaps, droop, lean, a double or broken top on some spruce and fir) at unit height and crown radius 1 from one plan per variant, so LOD0 (trunk + bent alpha cards, normals bent out from the trunk) and LOD1 (trunk + star-edged cones or blobs) share a silhouette. `materials.ts` holds the LOD hook: each LOD draws only in its band of distance from the camera (`kitUniforms.uLodCam`, so the shadow pass agrees) and crossfades with the next by a screen-door dither (Bayer 8x8, complementary cells); LOD0 sways (`TreeKit.setWind`). LOD2 (crossed quads) and the impostors (camera-facing quads) sample albedo and normal atlases baked from LOD0 (`TreeKit.bake`, 8 angles x one row per species, 1024 x 1280) and are lit in the shader like the standard material (sun, hemisphere, a quarter of the GGX specular, `FAR_GAIN`), so the colour holds across the swaps. Bands: LOD0 to 800 m on High, 400 m on Medium and 200 m on Low (BUG-107, `lod0ForDensity` from the Trees setting, in straight lines between; 80 m until then, and the cones past it read as stand-ins; `?lod0=<m>` sets it on every setting, up to 0.8 of the near radius), LOD1 to 400 m or to LOD0's edge where that is further (then no cones are drawn), LOD2 to 1.5 km, impostors to 4.5 km, scaled by the tree density setting (`setVegetationDensity`). `Vegetation` fills LOD0 and LOD1 from the trees near the camera (one InstancedMesh per species x variant, regathered every 25 / 60 m of camera movement through a 100 m sub-tile index per cell) and keeps one LOD2 and one impostor mesh per 1 km cell (all species, `iSpec` per instance). Culling (FEAT-158): before each draw `Vegetation.cull(camera, sun)` (Session's `draw`) keeps only the near sets' 25 m (LOD0) / 50 m (LOD1) tiles in view, or whose trees' shadows can reach into it (each tile's box stretched away from the sun by its tallest tree's shadow, up to 200 m), at the front of the instance buffers, and of those the tiles inside the sun's shadow camera first: the shadow pass draws only that prefix (`onBeforeShadow` swaps `mesh.count`), and of each LOD1 tree only the triangles that cast (a star cone's underside sits inside its shadow; `generate.ts` puts those last and `geometry.userData.castCount` says where they start). A set repacks only when a tile it needs is missing, with 15 / 40 m of slack so turning repacks a few times a second at most. A far mesh holds only the 100 m tiles of its cell within 150 m of its band and repacks once the camera has moved enough for the band to reach a tile it left out, so the cells round the camera no longer draw their every tree as a collapsed quad. The Low preset draws LOD0 to 200 m (`setVegetationBudget(near, far, 200)`); with `?lowgfx` (the software-GPU runs) it draws LOD1 from the camera out, no LOD0. Stand density (FEAT-074): a pack with `meta.stands` holds only the detected crowns, and a cell within 1 km past the near radius (1.5 km at full density, so 2.5 km) loads its `stands/<cx>_<cy>.png` and appends the stems it asks for (`trees/stands.ts`, `generateStems`: a position hash per 4 m cell, so the same stems every time) to its records, then rebuilds its LODs and shade entry. So they draw at every near LOD and as impostors in the mid ring, and nothing beyond, where the terrain's cover (which counts them) shades the forest. A cell drops them 250 m past that edge. The Forest setting (`forestDensity`, `setStandDensity`) thins only these stems; the detected crowns always draw. Generated stems are trunks in the collider only in the cells within 150 m of the rider. `vegetation.stats.stems` counts them. Crown width by openness (FEAT-298, `trees/widths.ts`): a pack with `meta.instances.crownWidths` (MapPacker's `pipeline/widths.py`) widens spruce, fir, Douglas-fir and hemlock that have open ground round them toward the lidar's open-grown 0.16 x height, keeps crowded ones at the species radius, gives each tree its own +-35 % draw, and thins the stems so the drawn canopy stays what it was. Its record field 4 is then the radius to draw (`crownRadius(..., drawn)`, kept within 0.5 to 3 of the species' own), a stem takes its cell's factor from B of the stand image's lower half times its own draw (`generateStems`), the kit shader keeps the trunk and the branches' roots at their natural width while the crown widens (`kCore`, `uKitCore`), and the crowns past the stems' reach grow by `STEM_GROW / sqrt(crownArea)`. Packs without the block draw as before. `tools/standcheck.mjs` checks the stems' widths. Tune the look with `tools/treeshot.mjs`, the cost with `tools/treeperf.mjs`.
- Far forest (FEAT-292, `world/trees/clumps.ts`, FEAT-278's pick; research in `docs/research/far-forest-canopy.md`): on the Low preset the forest past the trees' 600 m is drawn as clump impostors out to 9 km (`CLUMP_FAR_MIN`; `?clumpfar=<m>`). With the Trees setting (Medium, High) the per-tree trees keep their own reach and end, and the clumps only dissolve in over that end's fade (BUG-130: Drew judged High's own far trees better than the clumps at the game's camera): Medium's trees end over 4.9 to 6.1 km and the clumps run from there to 9 km; High's trees reach 9.7 km, so High draws no clumps and never bakes them (`LOW_REACH`, `CLUMP_MIN_BAND` in `Vegetation.ts`). This is on every pack with cover levels (`Vegetation.enableClumps`; without them, or with `?noclumps`, nothing changes). Bake: `ClumpCanopy` builds 8 stand patches (4 crown covers, 0.08 to 0.48, x 2 seeds; about 700 of the kit's own LOD0 trees) in the pack's species mix (`standMix`: the shares in `meta.vegetation`, the pack's trees and the far tree ring's averaged, species under 3% and krummholz left out; without them Colorado's spruce, fir and lodgepole, or bare hardwood and hemlock east of 100 W), and renders each from 5 view elevations into three 960 x 1536 atlases (albedo with coverage, normal with depth, snow mask with height and tree id; 24 MB with mips). It runs as steps inside `Vegetation.update` (a patch, then a tile per step, then the mips from an asynchronous read-back), a third of the trees' build budget a frame behind the loading screen, and `ClumpCanopy.busy` counts into `Vegetation.pending` so the loading screen and the still tools wait for it. Placement: per 1 km block, once, one clump per 64 m cell of forest from the cover levels' bytes (the pack level's B, where the drawn trees stand, else a level's forest share R, so a pack without lidar crowns places from its land cover) on the mid / far height sets, a millisecond a frame while riding; the blocks inside the trees' reach are placed too, so a Trees or quality change (Settings > Graphics > Apply) only repacks. Draw: one instanced draw, one camera-facing card per clump, blending the two tiles nearest its view elevation over the local slope, each texel thinned tree by tree against the cover at its own ground point (runs and glades cut clean) and written at its own depth. The card is rasterised on the camera side of everything its texels hold and the depth only moves away (`EXT_conservative_depth`, `depth_greater`), so a card behind a ridge fails the early depth test unshaded; without the extension every covered pixel is shaded (the `CONSERVATIVE` define). The material is a `RawShaderMaterial` (the extension directive has to come before three's prefix), so `syncOutput` sets its tone mapping, output transfer and fog defines from the renderer, the target and the scene the way three does for its own. The crown dither: where the atlas is minified the mips hold a stand's average, one tone and one normal that points up, which lit the stand like flat ground (43% of its noon light gone at dawn, where the drawn trees lose 20%). There a pixel belongs to one crown: crown cells on the card (`CROWN_W` 5 m by `CROWN_H` 16 m, doubling as they shrink to `CELL_PX` 2.4 px so they never get small enough to shimmer), each a tapered spire, round across with a lit and a shaded side, its own needle tone and snow share, and the pixel takes one of the two tiles with the blend as its chance. The stand stretches up to 2x by 3 km past the handover, as the kit's impostors grow with distance. On Low the per-tree impostors dissolve out over the same band the clumps dissolve in (`kit.bands.imp`) and no tree cell past it is loaded; on every preset that draws clumps the terrain's canopy lift starts at their far edge. In a pack with crown widths (FEAT-298) the stand patches widen their trees by the same rule (each tree's openness from its neighbours in the patch, the pack's multipliers), are thinned back to the crown area they had at the species radii, and stand shorter in the sparse classes (down to 0.65 of the height when fully open), so the forest keeps its shape at the handover. Snow (BUG-127): the snow atlas clears to a crown's mean cover under the cutout so its mips do not dilute a stand's snow by its gaps, and with a heavy load a crown's snow is not capped, so the far forest frosts with the near trees. `Vegetation.clumps.stats` has the bake and placement times and the clump count; `tools/clumpcheck.mjs` checks all of this, `tools/gpu_ab.mjs` the cost.
- Shrubs and understory (FEAT-159, `world/Shrubs.ts`, geometry in `world/trees/shrubs.ts`): the pack's shrub set (DATA-020, `shrubs/<cx>_<cy>.bin`: 0 willow, 1 juniper, 2 sagebrush, 3 young conifer, 4 Gambel oak) drawn within 300 m of the camera (scaled with the tree density setting) with the tree kit's atlas and material (`treeMaterial(..., { shrub: true })`: one more program, `SHRUB` defined). Willow is 10 to 12 straight red-orange stems as crossed strips (48 triangles), juniper a wide low dark dome and sagebrush a round grey-green one (36), Gambel oak 3 or 4 crooked grey stems each with a twig spray (40); young conifers are the kit's spruce or fir LOD1 (90), a little bushier than grown trees. One InstancedMesh per kind x variant, refilled from the shrubs in reach every 25 m of camera movement or when the snowpack changes; the band dithers them out at the edge. Burial: the terrain is the snow surface, so each shrub is sunk by the snowpack depth at its spot (`sim/Snowpack.ts`: a settled base by month, 1.45 m at 3,000 m in February, scaled by elevation from a third on the I-70 valley floor to 1.25 times from 3,200 m, less on steep south faces and more on north ones, plus the new snow and a fresh day's loose layer; +-10 % per shrub) and only what stands above it is drawn (`buryShrub`). A buried juniper, sagebrush or young conifer still shows a snow-covered hump of up to 0.25 to 0.4 m, fading out under 2 m more snow; willow and oak just go under. Per instance `aShrub` carries the snow line, where the material drifts snow round the stems (5 to 25 cm up), and the mound cover for a half-buried mat or sapling; the visible tops take the tree snow (FEAT-157) and no snow ghosts. Physics: a young conifer standing over 1.5 m above the snow gets a trunk collider like a tree; the rest is brush (`Collider.addBrush`, updated in place as the snow changes): `rider.surface.shrub` (0..1) adds `SHRUB_DRAG` (rider/tuning.ts, about 0.5 m/s through a willow clump at 12 m/s) and the brush sound (`Audio.shrubBrush`). Look with `treeshot.mjs --only=shrubs`, physics with `shrubcheck.mjs`, depth on the spot with `?snowdepth=`.
- Snow on the trees (FEAT-157, `world/trees/snow.ts`): one load for the day (`treeSnowState`, set from `RideFrame.streaming` through `TreeKit.setSnow`): 0.55 / 0.35 / 0.12 for fresh / mid-season / spring snow conditions, scaled by how deep in the winter the month is, rising to 1 with the shared new-snow cover (FEAT-105) after a storm; `melt` takes it back on the sun side of each crown from late morning on a clear day (much more in spring); `ghost` is 1 in deep winter. Each tree varies the load by +-15%, and above the snow-ghost band (`uGhostZ`, 3,450 to 3,560 m at treeline, jittered per tree; the pack's own treeline since DATA-021, and none on a pack with `treeline: null`, FEAT-234, forest to the top of the box like Boston Mills) a tree goes to a full load with rime on every face and its cards' alpha grown so the gaps fill in (its shadow fills in too). In LOD0 and LOD1 the snow sits on the upper side of what you see (the surface's own facing from screen derivatives, so the top of a card is white and its underside stays green, leaning on the crown's bent normal), times the vertex's `snowHold` from `generate.ts` (full at the base of a bough, 0.4 at its tip, none under the cones or on bark) and more on the lower tiers, broken up by a 0.3 m noise whose threshold falls as the load rises; LOD1's cones take 0.6 of the amount (`LOD1_SNOW`) so they match the cards. The bake adds a snow mask atlas (cover at load 0.5, at 1, on a ghost, and the ghost's filled silhouette), and the far shader blends bare, moderate and heavy by the tree's load. Snow uses the terrain's albedo and shade tint (`world/snowLook.ts`, shared with the tracks, the rider's snow cover and `TerrainMaterial`) with its normal pulled toward up. Needles get a fill from the snow under the crown and a back-lit translucency (`treeNeedleExtra`), so shaded trees read dark blue-green. No extra draw calls; texture memory 21.3 MB with the mask.
- Snow off the boughs (FEAT-217, `world/TreeBrush.ts`): each frame (view sync, render pose; not on a lift or with the camera detached) `Vegetation.crownContact` finds the needled crown the rider's body is deepest in: three heights on the body (0.3, 0.9, 1.6 m) against the generator's crown profile (`crownProfile`, from the species' crown base to the top), 0.35 m half width, full contact 0.8 m in; aspen, bare hardwood and snags hold no snow. The first touch of a tree drops up to 45 % of its near-side snow, riding on through it shakes more down with depth and speed, standing still drops nothing. The amount is the tree's load as the shader draws it (`treeSnowLoadAt` in `trees/snow.ts`: the day's load +-15 % or the snow-ghost load; nothing under 0.15, so none on a spring day) less half the sun-side melt, times the crown size; a tree remembers what it shed (the last 256) and builds back over 4 minutes; a replay or the rewind playing back shakes the trees without taking their snow (a memory of its own), so it drops what the live pass did. The snow goes out through the board's spray ring (`Spray.emit` / `commit`, no draw call of its own, at most 96 a frame): clumps (60 %) that fall from the contact height to 1.6 m above it on the near side of the crown and a fine cloud that hangs and drifts on the wind, both carried a little along with the rider; a touch also plays a soft thump and the hiss of it sifting down (`GameAudio.treePuff`). The unload: each frame the 8 shaken trees nearest the rider go to the tree shader (`uTreeShed` / `uTreeShedN` in `trees/snow.ts`: the base, the share of the near side gone, the contact height and the near side's direction; uniforms, so no per-instance data). The mesh LODs' vertex shader matches its instance's base against them (`treeSnowShed`, skipped when none) and the fragment shader takes the load off the near side, from 1.4 m under the contact height to about 2.5 m over it, soft at the edges; the bare side fills in as the tree builds back. A ghost's rime goes there too, though its shadow keeps the filled crown. The far LODs (past 400 m) don't show it. A slam: riding into a trunk (`Rider.lastHit` 'wall' or 'graze', with `Rider.hitSpeed` the speed into it, cleared each frame with `lastHit`) shakes the whole tree, which `Vegetation.trunkAt` finds (the nearest base within its collider radius plus 0.7 m, so a lodgepole's high crown counts too). From 2.5 m/s some of it comes off, from 7 m/s (where a square hit crashes) all of it: the tree is drawn bare on every side top to bottom (the second `uTreeShed` vec4's z), and its snow, in all four times a near side's, comes off the whole crown over the next frames at most 96 a frame, the clumps living long enough to fall to the snow. Checked by `treepuffcheck.mjs`.
- Riding: carving on a real edge angle (up to 72 deg on firm snow, 45 in powder) with the turn radius from the board's sidecut, washing into a skid past the grip; edge to edge takes about 0.3 s at speed; the rider leans into the hill up to 60 deg with deep toeside and heelside carve poses. Hard carve (FEAT-148, the squat key held while steering): `Rider.hardCarve` eases in, the edge cap goes to `HARD_PSI` (80 deg) and the edge rolls `HARD_ROLL` times faster (slowing with the square of the speed past `HARD_ROLL_V`), the lean may reach `HARD_PHI` and the snow holds up to `HARD_G` (2.6 g) times its grip; the press asks for the turn of the target edge, driven in over `HARD_DRIVE_T` once the edge is on the steer's side, while the edge holds only what its real angle balances, so a lagging edge (pressed from flat at speed, on ice) skids (`carveShort`, folded into `skid` for the drag, spray and sound) and short of more than `WASH_SHARE` (35%) of the turn asked, at speed, fills `washout` (a share, so softer snow that asks less washes out alike; the snow's grip scales what the edge holds; only deep powder, its share times depth over `HARD_DEEP` 30 cm, lowers the hard carve's edge cap and stops the washout); full, the rider crashes to the inside of the turn (`crashSide`, the ragdoll topples that way) and `onWashout` toasts it. `HARD_DRAG` adds a drag from the lateral acceleration. The view lays the body over to `LEAN_HARD` (73 deg) in the deep carve stance (BUG-066: the squat pose fades out while `hardCarve` is in, and `RiderView.deckDeficit` lifts the hips until the shins, knees and thighs clear the deck in the board's own frame, `DECK_CLEAR`) and drags the inside hand on the snow; the spray, the track depth and the carve hiss rise with it; `?debug` shows hard, short and wash. Also tuck, brake (the board swings across into a hockey stop, scrapes a wide track and holds on its edge once stopped; steering while braking digs the edge in for a harder stop or lets it off into a skidded turn or a side-slip), the fall line (a flat board follows its slide down the hill and side-slips when let go across it unless the uphill edge is held; on Realistic the edge catches when the board slides sideways onto its leading edge, or slides flat over rough snow; Settings > Gameplay > Fall line: Realistic (the default), Assisted, Off; the Feel presets set it, Arcade to Off), charged jumps with ollie and nollie off the board's spring (hold W / S with Space; the pop off flat snow is `JUMP_POP[Rider.jumpMode]` in `tuning.ts`, FEAT-311: Realistic is the high end of expert snowboarders' measured ollies, 0.43 m on a full charge, 0.6 m on a squat ollie and 0.16 m on a tap, so no flip off flat snow, and Arcade keeps the old 2.5 m; each mode also sets the squat and ollie multipliers, the pitch an ollie leaves and the air spin rate (Realistic 370 deg/s, so a full charge fits a 180 and nothing a flat 360; Arcade 200), and the launch on a slope (Realistic adds the pop to the velocity along the snow, so a pop clears the slope by the same 0.5 m at any speed; - Riding switch and stance (FEAT-139): `rider.heading` is always the steering axis along the direction of travel, and every physics value (edge, lateral g, lean, flip, brake side) is in its frame, so + edge is the heading's right-hand edge. Going backwards along it faster than `FAKIE_SPEED` (2.5 m/s), or landing with the board backwards, `turnRound()` adds PI to the heading and board yaw, negates the edge, lean and lateral g, and toggles `rider.switch`. `rider.goofy` comes from Settings > Gameplay > Stance. `rider.toeSign` is -1 when switch and goofy differ: `RiderView` then draws the board and body at the yaw + PI and negates every sideways and fore-aft value into the rig's frame (`fl`), so turning round never moves the rider on screen and the toe and heel poses stay the rider's own. A goofy rig (`buildRig(..., goofy)`) has the deck's nose at +Z and the +15 / -6 bindings swapped ends, so drawn turned round it is a true goofy stance. Seated and on foot are always drawn along the heading; unstrapping and strapping in turn the heading round when needed so nothing pops. Switch is 4 % less grip (`SWITCH_GRIP`), shows SWITCH on the HUD, and prefixes trick names ("Switch backside 180"). Replays and ghosts carry flag bits 8 (switch) and 16 (goofy). Skis never ride switch.
- Snow conditions (FEAT-053, `sim/SnowConditions.ts`): `snowAt()` turns the surface mask, the ground normal (slope and aspect), the elevation, the canopy and the day (`rider.snowWorld`: hour, storm, new snow cover, the Snow conditions setting, the pack's elevation range, set by `main.ts` each frame) into weights for groomed / powder / chop / ice / slush / rock that sum to 1, plus the physics they blend (`grip`, `drag0`, `drag1`, `turn`, `jolt`, `skidScrub`, `depth`). Powder keeps in the shade of north aspects, high up, under the trees and after a storm (a storm or a powder day makes the groomers ride soft too); open off-piste is tracked out into chop as the day goes on; wind scours high exposed ground to ice and spring mornings refreeze the groomers; the afternoon sun makes slush low down on the sunny faces (most of all in spring). In `Rider.ts` the state scales the grip (ice about half), the sidecut's arc and the lean's turn force (powder 0.7: wider, slower turns), the snow drag (powder speed-scaled until the board planes at `PLANE_SPEED`, the nose sinking and digging in below `SINK_SPEED`, slush heavy), how much a skid scrubs speed (ice little), and chop adds seeded random jolts to the heading and a sideways kick (`CHOP_*`, `rider.chop` for the camera). `bootSink` reads the depth on foot. The spray (`RiderView.updateSpray`: a plume off the edge in powder, almost none on ice), the camera rumble, the track depth and the sound (`Audio.update`: ice on the hardpack layer, slush a wet hush, chop a crackle; footsteps too) follow the weights. The HUD names the dominant kind while riding and shows the kind and the boot sink on foot; `?debug` adds the weights. FEAT-221 (`ui/Conditions.ts`, `conditionsText()` from `ui/HudLine.ts`) turns the riding tag into a plain-words readout: the kind, the depth for powder (and for chop or slush past 10 cm) in 2 in steps (5 cm in metric), `Moguls 4 ft` in front on a bump field (`surface.moguls` 0.3 or more, the size `mogulSize()` at the slope, eased over 1.5 s so the bump faces do not swing it, 0.5 ft or 0.1 m steps), and at most two tags, first match wins: edge letting go (`washout`), bogging down (powder, under 0.6 x `PLANE_SPEED`), nose sinking (`noseSink`), floating (powder at `PLANE_SPEED` or more), edges skittering (`snow.grip` under 0.75), bumpy (`snow.jolt`), heavy and slow (slush), catchy (`catchRisk`), brushing shrubs (`surface.shrub`), in the trees (`surface.canopy`). - Deformable snow and powder spray (FEAT-044, design in `docs/research/deformable-snow-and-spray.md`): a hybrid. The sharp trench is the ribbon (`Tracks.ts`): in soft snow its floor is 0.7 of the loose snow depth (8 to 25 cm in powder, the 5 cm groove on a groomer) and the knife-cut collapse is held back, so a powder carve leaves a trench the board's width, never wider. `world/Deform.ts` renders the track lanes and the boot prints top-down every frame into a 512 px render target over a 64 m window that follows the rider (leading along the travel direction), with MAX blending (R depression, G pushed-up snow, B packed); the lanes and prints are the persistence, so the texture holds no state. Shaders read it only through its mipmap at about 1 m per texel (`deformSag`), so the GPU pre-blurs the stamp to the terrain's 1 m vertex spacing and it cannot alias: the terrain (`TerrainMaterial.ts`), the ribbon and the boot prints all sink by that soft sag at their vertices, and the terrain tilts its normal by the sag's slope. A first build displaced the terrain from the full-resolution texture on a 25 cm split; it beaded along diagonal tracks and cost about 120 draw calls, so it was dropped. `world/Spray.ts` replaces the CPU spray: a ring of 8,192 GPU particles moved analytically in the vertex shader from spawn records (wisps on groomers and ice, clouds that hang, grow and drift on the wind in powder and slush, a low sheet in a hockey stop, a burst on landing), emission from speed x sin(edge angle) x loose depth, the skid and the scrape. Settings > Graphics > Snow dents turns the sag off; the Low preset and `?nodeform` turn it off too; Medium draws it at half the texture over a 48 m window.
- Board and boots set down into loose snow (FEAT-152, `world/SnowCover.ts`, `RiderView.setDown`): from 5 cm of loose snow (`rider.snow.depth`; all in by 10 cm, `buryGate`, so groomers at 2.5 cm, ice and rock are untouched and any powder, 12 cm or more, is all in) the board is drawn tail low and nose up (`buryShape`): the tail sinks 0.8 of the depth when slow, 0.45 by `PLANE_SPEED` and no more than 4 cm by 22 m/s, never past 0.9 of it; the nose floats at 0.1 of the depth when slow and at the surface once planing, up to 12 deg nose-up; slow, the nose dig (`noseSink`) takes the nose down to the tail and past it. The rider is lowered by the board's middle along the snow normal and pitched about it, easing in and out over about 0.3 s (up out of it in the air, snapped back on a chair or in a crash). Visual only: the physics, tracks, cameras and spray keep the snow surface. Every rider material gets a snow-cover term (`addSnowCover`): below a plane through the untouched snow under the rider, the fragment takes the snow's albedo, roughness and normal, with a noisy edge from 2 cm under to 4 cm over the surface; the terrain hides what is deeper. The deformation sag is held back ahead of and beside the buried board (`setDeformHold`, from `riderView.powderHold` in `main.ts`), so the snow wall around the nose and the boots stays while the trench opens behind. `Spray.deepPowder` throws a bow wave off the nose at speed and spills snow over the tail when slow. Presses in deep powder (scaled by powder x depth/0.3): in `Rider` a lean forward digs the nose in more and for longer (`SINK_NOSE`, `SINK_NOSE_SPEED`) and a lean back lifts it out (`SINK_TAIL`); the press drag is 1.5 times more on a nose press and gone on a tail press (`PD_NOSE_DRAG`, `PD_TAIL_EASE`); the press balance pulls a nose press on toward its nose (`PB_POWDER`) and gives a tail press less drift, jitter and runaway (`PB_POWDER_STEADY`, `PB_POWDER_CALM`). The press tip and bend apply on top of the set-down, so a tail press lifts the nose out of the cover; the held sag moves 0.35 m toward the end that is down (`HOLD_SHIFT`); the spray follows it: a tail press cuts the bow wave and throws a rooster tail off the tail, a nose press throws more bow wave and spills over the nose. On foot the soles sit down by the whole boot sink in loose snow (`GaitInput.soleShare`, 0.6 of it on firm snow) and the snow covers the boots and shins. The bow wave and the spill are powder only, smaller in shallow powder.

### States

The session is a state machine (`core/StateMachine.ts`, FEAT-162). One state is active; it decides which systems tick and it takes the input, so a menu and the riding keys never both see a key.

| state | entered | who moves the rider | keys it takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `loading` | the session starts | nobody (no frame runs: the loop starts when it ends) | none |
| `attract` (main menu) | after loading with a menu; Quit to main menu | nobody, hidden (`menu`) | none: input is suspended, the menu has it |
| `riding` | RIDE; leaving any state below | the physics (`ride`) | every riding action; Esc pauses |
| `rewind` | the rewind key held while riding | the recording (`recording`) | the rewind key; Esc lands where it got to, then pauses |
| `paused` | Esc while riding | nothing ticks; the frame only renders | Esc resumes; the pause menu's own actions |
| `replay` (K) | K, or the pause menu's Replay | the recording (`recording`) | the viewer's keys; Esc, K or Circle close it |
| `map` (M) | the full map opening, however (M, the pause menu, RIDE's "or drop in") | nobody, though a lift carries on (`hold`) | the map's (mouse, touch, stick, + / -); Esc, M or Circle close it; the music key |

A state that changes the state inside its update hands the rest of that frame to the new state (its update runs next with the same dt), so Esc closing the map or K opening the viewer acts in the frame the key went down. The Map state follows `ResortMap.onOpenChange`, so a drop-in click, a tool calling `__sim.map.close()` or the pause menu all move it. While Outrun's monster has the rider (`caught`) the eat sequence moves it, whatever the state asked. `__sim.state` is the active state's name; `__sim.attract` is still there.

### The frame, in order

`Session.ts` `tickFrame(dt)` reads the devices (`RideFrame.readInput`: `controls.update`, the right stick and gyro into the mouse look), then runs the active state's update and render. Paused renders and closes the input frame. Every other state reads its own keys, then runs `RideFrame.update` and `draw`, which do these in this order every drawn frame (FEAT-160, FEAT-162). Keep the order when moving code: the view steps draw the pose the sim steps just made, the cameras read the rider view (the eye), and streaming and the sky follow the final camera.

1. The state's keys: riding's restart hold, trial key (`modes.trialKey`), replay and rewind (they switch state), camera cycle, map, piste, track, fly, strap in or out, boost (`modes.boostKey`); the replay viewer's keys and the replayed pose; the rewind's scrub and pose; the map's stick, zoom and drop-in; behind the main menu the input is suspended and the backdrop camera drifts.
2. The touch buttons shown or hidden; the eye camera's photo look (no rider, spray, markers or piste lines).
3. Simulation on the sim pose, as far as the state's drive allows (`RUNS` in `RideFrame.ts`): lift ride, then the rider (its own 1/120 s fixed steps), replay recording, run stats, rumble. The lift ride, Outrun (step 5) and the monsters step on the frame `dt`, not the fixed clock: moving them onto it would change their results (FEAT-166).
4. View sync on the render pose (interpolated between the last two physics steps): rider view, snow off the trees the rider brushes (FEAT-217), tracks, prints, puffs. Then the sim pose comes back.
5. Modes (`modes.update`, FEAT-164), each one's update then its HUD, in this order: Free ride (nothing); Outrun: its sim, its events emitted on the bus (sound, rumble, camera kick, toast and the eat start subscribe), the countdown beeps, the eat sequence, the monsters, the Outrun HUD; Time trials: the sim, gates, ghost, route walls, the trial HUD.
6. Audio (reads the sim pose and the lift).
7. Cameras on the render pose: free, eye, first person, replay trackside, else the chase camera; then `modes.afterCamera`: the eat sequence overrides it and the chase sounds take it as their listener; lens, goggles, speed lines. The press meter projects through the final camera.
8. Sky and effects from the camera: shadows (CSM), clouds, snowfall, particle light, tree fade (FEAT-155), the deformation stamp (FEAT-044).
9. Streaming around the rider (or the camera when detached): terrain, prefetch, trees, rocks; snow cover, the skied-out day, moguls; roads, the chair, lifts, bridges, lift lines, landmarks; the live sun.
10. Prompt and key hints, the sky's follow, tile eviction, the map (the full map while it is open, else the minimap), horizon labels.
11. Render, then the P-key shot (the drawing buffer is only readable in the same task), `controls.endFrame`, and the replay overlay restored.
12. Frame rate and the HUD line.

### Inside the rider step and the rider view

Each rider step (`Rider.update`, in step 3 above, FEAT-165): the squat and press lean ease, the press balance (`Press.ts`), then the surface and snow under the rider, then one of strapping in or out, walking (`OnFoot.ts`), the crash slide, the air (`Jumps.ts` `airStep`, landing in `Tricks.ts`), or on the snow (`Ride.ts` `rideStep`): gravity along the slope, the edge and carve (`EdgeModel.ts` `carve`, which ends the step on a washout), the brake (`Brake.ts`), the sideways grip and edge catch (`EdgeModel.ts` `grip`), the drag, the push and glide, the jump, following the ground, the collisions.

The rider view (`RiderView.update`, in step 4 above): on foot it hands over to `FootPose.ts`; riding, the orientation and the set-down into powder (`Effects.ts`), the inclination (`BodyPose.ts` `incline`), the board's roll and flex (`BoardFlex.ts`), the ragdoll if crashed, the pose blend and physical layer (`BodyPose.ts`), FK, legs, head and hands (`Limbs.ts` `settleBody`), the spray.

### Events

Cross-system news goes through `core/Events.ts` (FEAT-163): `events.on(type, fn)` (returns its `off`), `events.off`, `events.emit({ type, ... })`, with every event in the one `GameEvent` union. `emit` calls the listeners at once, in the order they subscribed, so a listener runs inside the frame step that emitted (the list above still holds). The sim classes keep their own hooks (`rider.onTrick`, `trial.onEvent`, ...) because the node checks drive them without a bus; `session/eventSources.ts` points those hooks at `emit`, the frame emits the landmarks it pulls and `OutrunMode` the Outrun events.

| event | emitted by | listeners |
|---|---|---|
| `trick`, `washout`, `pressEnd` | `Rider` | toast |
| `footstep` | `Rider` (skate push), `RiderView` (foot plant) | audio |
| `footPlant` | `RiderView` | boot prints |
| `trial` (armed, start, split, finish, cancel) | `TimeTrial` | trial panel, then chime, then rumble |
| `runEnd` | `RunStats` | run card |
| `landmark` | the frame (`landmarks.maintain`) | toast |
| `song` | `GameAudio` | toast |
| `outrun` | `OutrunMode.update` (`outrun.events`) | toast (second monster), audio, rumble, then `OutrunMode.listen`: camera kick (footfalls), eat start (caught, after the audio) |
| `eatSound`, `treeCrack` | `Eat`, `Monsters` | audio (the chase sounds) |

A new event: add it to the union, emit it where the news is known, subscribe where it is used (`ui/HudEvents.ts`, `GameAudio.listen`, `PadFeedback.listen`, the session for the world, or the mode's own `listen`). Landings, crashes and lift boarding are not events yet: audio, rumble, the camera and the spray each read the rider's state in their own update, on different rules, so one event would change when they fire.

## Test tools (`game/tools/`)

- `get_pack.mjs <slug> [--force] [--sha256] [--catalog <url|file>] [--packs <dir>]`, `--list` and `--save-catalog <file>` (FEAT-222; `npm run get <slug>`) installs a published pack: reads `catalog.json` from the resorts repo through the GitHub contents API (BUG-096: current right after a publish, one of the 60 unauthenticated API calls an hour; the raw URL GitHub's CDN caches for 5 minutes is the fallback on any API failure, with a warning; with `GITHUB_TOKEN` set the API call sends it, and only the API call (BUG-109: CI's jobs, off the unauthenticated limit); `SA_CATALOG` or `--catalog` reads that URL or file as is), finds the slug in its resorts or its `test` list (FEAT-241: test packs such as `synth`, one per pack format, never shown in the picker; it takes the one at this game's `PACK_FORMAT`), downloads the release zip in Node (release assets send no CORS headers), checks the sha256 and that the pack's format is this game's `PACK_FORMAT`, unzips into `packs/.<slug>.incoming`, swaps it in and rewrites `packs/index.json`. The swap (this and `--from`'s) tries a rename Windows refuses with EPERM, EBUSY or EACCES again, 6 tries over about 10 s, saying it is waiting (BUG-095: Defender scanning the fresh tiles, or something holding the old pack), and then puts the old pack back. An installed pack carries `packs/<slug>/.catalog.json` (release version, sha256 and, since FEAT-248, the catalog's `status`, which `localPacks` lists for the Preview badge), so a newer release replaces it on its own; a pack built on this PC has none and is only replaced with `--force`, as is a `--from` copy, and a junction is never replaced. `--from <dir>` (FEAT-242; `npm run get [<slug>] -- --from <dir>`) downloads nothing: it copies a pack built in the MapPacker checkout into `packs/<slug>` as the release zip would carry it (no `debug/`, no dot files), marks it with `.from.json` (source folder and `build`), swaps it in and rewrites `packs/index.json`, to ride a build before it is published. `<dir>` is the pack folder or the `packs/` folder holding `<slug>/`; the slug defaults to the pack's `meta.json` and must match it, and the pack must be this game's `PACK_FORMAT`. It replaces a link (only the link goes), a catalog pack or an earlier copy, and a pack built in this checkout only with `--force` (deleted); the same build again is skipped. A copy, not a link, because `git worktree remove` follows a junction under the ignored `packs/` and empties the folder it points at. `npm run get -- --list` shows a copy as `copy of <dir>`. `--sha256` prints the sha256 of the zip it would install and installs nothing (CI's cache key). `--save-catalog <file>` writes the catalog it read to the file and installs nothing (BUG-109: CI's one read of it, so the key and the download name the same release). The dev and host servers call it for the resort picker (`vite.config.ts` `packDownloads`: `GET /__packs/catalog`, `POST /__packs/get`, `GET /__packs/job?slug=`; the panel is `src/ui/ResortPicker.ts`); the listing's catalog is cached for 5 minutes, a Download reads it afresh so it installs the newest release. The panel shows Update for a pack with `.catalog.json` that is an older release or an older format than the catalog's entry at this `PACK_FORMAT`, and "needs rebuild" for an older-format pack without one, which `get` would refuse (BUG-106).

## Releases

`.github/workflows/release.yml` (FEAT-262) makes a release of the Windows zip. It runs only by hand: Actions > Release > Run workflow > pick the branch (`main`) and `bump` (`patch`, the default; `minor` or `major`) > Run workflow. Merges to `main` never make a release, and nothing in `.github/workflows/` runs on tags or releases.

The release tag is the version of record: `X.Y.Z`, no `v`. `0.0.1` is the first release (made by hand, no zip) and the starting point, so the first `patch` run makes `0.0.2`. A run:
1. reads the latest release's tag (`gh release view`, which skips drafts and pre-releases) and bumps it: from `0.0.1`, `patch` is `0.0.2`, `minor` `0.1.0`, `major` `1.0.0`. A tag that isn't `X.Y.Z` stops the run.
2. stops before building when the new tag or a release by that name (a draft too) already exists, so a release is never overwritten, or when the latest release is already this commit (a second click with nothing merged in between).
3. builds at the commit it ran on with Node 24 (the exe embeds the `node.exe` that builds it): `npm ci`, then `npm run package -- --version X.Y.Z` in `game/`, which stamps the version into the exe (`SnowboardAnywhere.exe --version`) and the main menu. `game/package.json`'s `version` is not used.
4. `gh release create X.Y.Z game/release/SnowboardAnywhere-win.zip --target <commit> --generate-notes --notes-start-tag <last tag>`: the tag, the release with the zip attached, and notes listing the PRs merged since the last release. The run's summary links it.

Runs queue (`concurrency: release`) instead of racing for the same number. It needs only `contents: write`: it makes a tag and a release, no commits, so the `main` ruleset doesn't apply. The tag is made last, with the release, so a failed build leaves nothing behind; a failed upload can leave a draft release, which the next run stops on (delete it under Releases). Release assets can be up to 2 GB each; the zip is about 185 MB. The repo is public, so anyone can download a release. `PLAY.md` sends riders to `releases/latest`, and FEAT-263 (#590) will read that for updates.

## Accounts (FEAT-273)

Signing in is optional. A guest rides everything; the main menu's corner line reads "Playing as guest" with a Sign in link, and shows the name (and the player number, when there is one) with an Account link once signed in. Both links open the account panel (`ui/AccountPanel.ts`) in the menu's pop-up. Accounts exist so a signed-in maintainer can start a resort build from the game (FEAT-246) and so multiplayer (FEAT-021) has identities. Sign-in methods: GitHub, and (FEAT-274) email and password. Firebase Authentication holds the accounts and the passwords (the game never stores a password); the account id is the Firebase uid.

**First-time setup (BUG-119).** Once per PC, in this order, before the first sign-in:
1. **`game/.env.local` in each checkout** (main and every worktree): copy `game/.env.example`, fill in the four `VITE_FIREBASE_*` values and set `SBA_FIREBASE_ADMIN_KEY` to the service-account key (Web config per checkout, below). Without it the menu says "Sign-in is not set up in this build." and Sign in is disabled.
2. **Publish the database rules:** paste `game/firebase/database.rules.json` into the Firebase console under Realtime Database > Rules and click Publish. `cd game && node tools/set_role.mjs --check` then prints `index ok: players/player`; without the rules it prints the fix and exits 1.
3. **Sign in once** in the game at `http://localhost:5173`, so the account exists.
4. **`node tools/set_role.mjs --email <you> --role maintainer --player 1`** (`set_role.mjs`, below).
5. **Sign out and back in**, so the new claims are in the token.
6. **Pull the MapPacker clone** (`git pull` in `MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere`, or `SBA_MAPPACKER`): Find a resort needs `resort.py search` (MapPacker FEAT-279). An older clone gives "The MapPacker clone at <path> has no resort search yet: pull its main."

**Email and password (FEAT-274).** An account can have either method or both, and both open the same account (same uid, same claims, so a maintainer is a maintainer either way and Build it works the same).
- A guest's panel: Sign in with GitHub, or an email and password with Sign in or Make an account. Enter in either field presses Sign in (MainMenu's pop-up reads the field's `data-submit`).
- **Verification.** An account whose only method is an email and password that was never verified does not sign in: Make an account creates it, sends Firebase's verification email and ends the session ("Account made. We sent a link to ..."); signing in before the link is used sends a new link and ends the session again ("Verify your email first ..."). A restored session of such an account is signed out too. An account that also has GitHub is not held back (GitHub opened it), so adding a password to a GitHub account works at once; the add still sends a verification email when the address is not verified. The dev server holds the same line: `heldForVerification` in `tools/accounts/verifier.mjs` refuses a token whose only identity is an unverified email (401), so an account made through Firebase's REST API cannot use the account routes either.
- **Adding a method.** A second method joins an account only from a session already signed in to it: the signed-in panel lists the account's methods and offers the missing one under "Add a sign-in method" (`AccountService.addMethod`: `linkWithCredential` with `EmailAuthProvider.credential` for a password, `linkWithPopup` for GitHub). The email field starts with the account's email.
- **Never joined by a matching email.** With one account per email on, a sign-up (or a GitHub sign-in) whose email belongs to another account is refused with "That email already belongs to another account. Sign in the way you usually do, then add this method from your account." Adding a method whose email or GitHub identity opens another account is refused the same way ("... so it cannot be added to this one"); neither account changes.
- **Email enumeration protection** is on (Firebase's default for new projects), so a wrong password and an email with no account both come back as `auth/invalid-credential`: the game shows one line for both ("That email and password do not open an account. Check them, or sign in the way you usually do and add a password from your account."), which also covers someone trying a password on a GitHub-only account. There is no password reset yet.

**The two sides.**
- The game (browser): `src/account/` and `src/firebase/` (see Code layout). `AccountService` gives the state (`pending`, `guest`, `signed-in`) and does sign in and sign out; `isMaintainer(state)` is false for a guest, a pending state and any non-maintainer. The Firebase SDK is a lazy chunk and loads only when the web config is complete and the page is not `?headless`, so CI builds (no config) never load it. In dev, `window.__account` exposes the service.
- The dev server (Node): `tools/accounts/`. `GET /api/account` (dev server only, never `npm run host` or the launcher) takes `Authorization: Bearer <ID token>`, checks it with `verifyIdToken(token, true)` (revocation checked) and answers `{ uid, role, player }`: a guest gets 401, a signed-in non-maintainer `role: 'player'`, a maintainer `role: 'maintainer'`. Every account route refuses a foreign `Origin` first (`refuseForeignOrigin`, BUG-110). A route that needs a role is wrapped: `requireAccount(verifier, handler, { role: 'maintainer' })` (guest 401, wrong role 403).
- FEAT-246's routes (`tools/resort_build_routes.mjs`, below) wrap their handlers in `requireAccount(devVerifier(), ..., { role: 'maintainer' })` and send `account.uid` as `requested_by`. They accept only the `Authorization: Bearer` token, never a cookie or any other ambient credential: Vite runs these routes before its DNS-rebinding guard, so the bearer token is the defence. `FirebaseAccount.idToken()` returns null when the user changed (a sign-out) while the token was read, so a build request never carries the previous user's token.

**Find a resort and Build it (FEAT-246, EPIC-17).** The resort picker (`ui/ResortPicker.ts`, on the main menu's Change resort and the first-run screen) has a Find a resort field that filters the built and catalog rows by name (any case, no accents) for everyone. For a signed-in maintainer (`isMaintainer`), a query of 3 or more characters also calls `GET /api/resorts/search?q=` after a 300 ms pause, with the ID token as `Authorization: Bearer`, and adds each match whose slug the list doesn't have, with its region, country, runs, lifts and Build it. Build it posts `{ slug, name, osm_id }` to `POST /api/resorts/build`; the row says Build started and stays for the session, until the build watcher lists its run (Build progress, below). A guest or player sends nothing; a server without the routes (`npm run host`, the launcher: not JSON, or a 404) gives the filter only, with no error. Dev server only (`vite.config.ts` `accountRoutes`, `configureServer`):
- `GET /api/resorts/search?q=` (3 to 100 characters) runs `python pipeline/resort.py search --json -- <q>` in the MapPacker clone (MapPacker FEAT-279; read-only, about 1 s with the OpenSkiData index cached) with `execFile` and an argument array, 90 s at most (the first search downloads the 21 MB index when the cache has none), and passes its JSON through: `{ query, matches: [{ openskidata_id, osm_id, name, region, country, lat, lon, runs, lifts, status, slug, configured, sold_with }], skipped, closest }`. The clone is `SBA_MAPPACKER` (in `game/.env.local`, like `SBA_FIREBASE_ADMIN_KEY`; the dev server loads every `SBA_` name from it), else `MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere` next to the game's main checkout (also from a worktree). Python is `SBA_PYTHON`, else `python`. The index is MapPacker's: `<SBA_RAW_ROOT>/_index`, or `SBA_INDEX_DIR`. No clone, no Python: 503 with a one-line reason; a clone with no `search` command (older than FEAT-279: argparse's `invalid choice: 'search'`): 502 "The MapPacker clone at <path> has no resort search yet: pull its main."; resort.py failing otherwise: 502 with its last error line; over the time limit: 504.
- `POST /api/resorts/build` `{ slug, name, osm_id }` (at most 4 KB) checks each against the shapes MapPacker's `build_pack_ci.py inputs` accepts (slug: lower-case letters, digits and single hyphens, at most 60; name: 1 to 100 characters, no control characters, not starting with `-`; osm_id: `123`, `way/123` or `relation/123`, or `skimap/123` for a ski area OpenSkiData knows only from Skimap.org, MapPacker FEAT-286), a 400 before any run otherwise, then runs `gh workflow run build-pack.yml -R drewkeriazes/MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere -f slug=... -f name=... -f osm_id=... -f requested_by=<account.uid>` (Drew's `gh`, signed in; `-f` takes the values as raw strings). 202 `{ slug }`; the same slug again within 10 minutes from this server is a 409 (a double click never queues two builds), unless the builds route has seen that build end since (it failed, or published nothing: Build it again works at once); `gh` failing is a 502 with its error, no `gh` a 503. A run from Drew's `gh` is a trusted publish: it builds, releases and merges by itself, so no check ever calls the real route, and the first real Build it is Drew's.
- `GET /api/resorts/builds` (FEAT-247, same gate) answers `{ builds: [{ slug, runId, url, state, stage?, why?, prUrl?, at, doneAt? }], checkedAt }`, newest first: the `build-pack` runs started in the last 2 days that have not finished, and those that finished in the last hour (a run whose publish PR is still open stays listed; a merged or closed one counts from then). `url` is the run, `prUrl` its publish PR, `at` when the run started, `doneAt` when it ended. It runs `gh run list --workflow build-pack.yml -R drewkeriazes/MapPacker-SnowboardAnywhere --created >=<2 days ago> --json databaseId,displayTitle,status,conclusion,createdAt,updatedAt,url` (the slug from the run name `Build pack <slug>`), `gh run view <id> --json jobs` for each run not finished (and once for each finished run that did not succeed, to say where it stopped), and `gh pr list --label publish --state all` (head `publish/<slug>-v<N>`), all with `execFile` and argument arrays. One answer serves every request for 10 s (a request while `gh` is still answering waits for the same one), so several tabs polling cost no more `gh` calls; a Build it clears it. No `gh`: 503; `gh` failing: 502 with its last error line.

**Build progress (FEAT-247).** The states (`tools/build_progress.mjs`, over MapPacker's three jobs `queue`, `probe` and `build` and the build job's steps named after the stages), in order:

| state | label in the picker | when |
|---|---|---|
| `queued` | Queued | the `queue` job has not finished (an earlier build is running), or no job has started |
| `probe` | Checking data hosts | the `probe` job |
| `building` | Building: resolve / fetch / build / report / publish | the `build` job; `stage` is its first stage step not finished (the App token steps count as publish) |
| `publishing` | Publishing | the run succeeded and a publish PR for the slug, opened after the run started (and before the slug's next run), is open |
| `published` | Published | that PR merged |
| `no-change` | No change | the run succeeded and opened no publish PR (the pack equals the published one, MapPacker FEAT-245) |
| `failed` | Failed at <stage>, Failed checking data hosts, Cancelled while queued, Failed: publish PR closed | the run failed or was cancelled (`why` is its conclusion, `stage` where it stopped), or the publish PR closed unmerged (`why: 'pr-closed'`) |

The page's watcher (`ui/BuildWatch.ts`), started from `Game.start`, asks the route on load and then every 15 s while a build is open (queued to publishing, or a Build it from this page whose run has not shown up yet, for up to 3 minutes), whether the picker is open or not, so a build started before the tab closed shows again when the game reopens. It asks nothing for a guest or a player, or on a build without the dev server (`import.meta.env.DEV` false: `npm run host`, the launcher); a server that answers the route with anything but its JSON turns it off for the page, quietly. A failed request asks again a minute later; signing out stops it and forgets the builds. The picker draws each build on its resort's row (the Build it row, or the Ride / Download row of a rebuilt resort, or a row named from the slug for a build the page didn't start), with the run or publish PR linked on the line under it, and redraws on `onChange` while it is open; a build that failed can be built again from its search match. `onChange(cb)` gets every build and what changed (`{ build, prev }`, `prev` missing the first time a run is seen); `onPublished(cb)` fires once per run when a build seen open on this page turns Published. A build already Published when the page loads comes through `onChange` only (FEAT-248 follows `onChange`, so it covers both). On Published the picker also asks for the catalog again.

**A published build is ready (FEAT-248).** `ui/ReleaseToast.ts`, made in `Game.start` (not headless), listens to the watcher's `onChange`. A run that turns Published (or that the page first sees Published, having finished within the last hour, `RECENT_MS`) is downloaded with the picker's routes: `POST /__packs/get {slug}` (it reads the catalog afresh, BUG-096), then `GET /__packs/job?slug=` every second, the toast showing the progress. A job that fails because the catalog does not list the release yet, or could not be read, is started again 30 s later, 4 times at most. Once it is in, `GET /__packs/catalog` gives the pack's name, its `formatVersion` (from `local`) and its `status`: a format other than `PACK_FORMAT` (or a download `get_pack.mjs` refused for its format) gives a toast that says "needs a newer game" or "needs rebuild" and has no Ride; otherwise "<Resort> is ready" with Ride, which is `leaveTo(packHref(slug, location.href))`: the same tab with `pack` set and `x`, `y`, `h` dropped, as the picker's own Ride, with no leave prompt mid-ride. A `preview` pack carries the Preview badge (`PREVIEW_BADGE` in `ui/ResortPicker.ts`); the picker's rows and the main menu's resort card show it too, the card from `GET /__packs/local` (asked only on the dev server, `npm run host` and the launcher, so a static server logs no 404). The download done, the page sends the `sba-pack-installed` window event (`PACK_INSTALLED`) and an open picker reads the catalog listing again, so the resort is a Ride row. No change and Failed get a toast with the run (or publish PR) link and no Ride. A Published run of the pack the page is riding is not downloaded under the running game: its toast offers Download and ride, which downloads it and then reloads into it (no swap mid-ride: FEAT-280). One toast per run: the run ids of the toasts shown are kept in `localStorage` `releaseToasts` (the last 50; a page's own copy when storage throws), and a run the page is already handling is skipped. The toasts (`#release-toasts`, z-index 25: over the menu and the pause menu, under the loading screen and the settings) stay until closed or clicked; a closed toast comes back only as another kind (closed while downloading, it shows again when ready). They never take the focus: their buttons are not tab stops, a mousedown on them is prevented, and PadNav and MenuNav do not walk into them, so the keys and a controller keep steering.

**Roles and numbers.** The role is the custom claim `role: 'maintainer'` (that exact string); the player number is the claim `player: <integer>`. Anything else, or missing, is role `player`, on both sides. Both are set only with `set_role.mjs`. A player number is a label: it can be reassigned by running the script again, 0 is a valid label, and no number is the normal state (most accounts have none, and nothing treats that as an error; compare with `!== null`, never by truthiness). Drew is player 1. Automatic numbers are deferred to FEAT-021. `players/<uid>` in the Realtime Database holds `player`, `displayName` and `role`.

**Where each secret lives.**

| what | where | never in |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub OAuth client secret | the Firebase console only (Authentication > Sign-in method > GitHub) | the repo, any file on a PC |
| Firebase service-account key | `SBA_FIREBASE_ADMIN_KEY` in `game/.env.local` (gitignored; Drew's key sits under `~/.config/snowboardanywhere/`, and `*firebase-adminsdk*.json` is gitignored too), else `%USERPROFILE%\.snowboardanywhere\firebase-admin-key.json` | any checkout, `game/src`, `dist/`, the launcher, the release zip |

| The signed-in player's ID token | Firebase's own browser storage | anywhere the game writes |

**Web config per checkout.** Copy `game/.env.example` to `game/.env.local` in each checkout and fill in the four values (Firebase console > Project settings > Your apps). A worktree without its own `.env.local` has no config: the corner line shows Sign in disabled and the game plays as guest. Release builds (`release.yml`, which runs `npm run package`) read the same four names from the repository variables (Settings > Secrets and variables > Actions > Variables); with no variables the release simply has no sign-in.

**Sign-in works on `localhost` only.** Firebase checks the page's domain against Authentication > Settings > Authorized domains: `localhost`, `snowboardanywhere.firebaseapp.com` and `snowboardanywhere.web.app`. The browser API key is restricted to the websites `http://localhost:5173/*`, `http://localhost/*` (port 80) and `https://snowboardanywhere.firebaseapp.com/*`. So open the game at `http://localhost:5173` (the launcher or `npm run dev`); a dev server on another port, or the launcher's fallback port, is refused until that port is added to the key's website list (Google Cloud console > APIs & Services > Credentials). `npm run host` from another computer is not an authorized domain. From any such address the game shows "Sign-in works only at http://localhost:5173 on this PC. Here you can play as a guest." (Firebase reports it as `auth/requests-from-referer-<origin>-are-blocked.`). A bad or missing key shows "Sign-in is not set up correctly in this build."

**`set_role.mjs`.**

```
cd game
node tools/set_role.mjs --email you@example.com --role maintainer --player 1 --name "Drew"
node tools/set_role.mjs --uid <uid> --role player
node tools/set_role.mjs --check
```

`--uid` or `--email` picks the account (it must have signed in once). `--role` is `maintainer` or `player` (`player` removes the claim); `--player <n>` is an integer 0 or more; `--name` sets the display name in `players/<uid>` (default: the account's own). Other claims are kept. A number another account holds is refused (exit 1, naming that uid): to move a number, rerun the script for that uid with another number first. Rerunning for the same uid replaces its number. A demotion (maintainer to player) also revokes the account's refresh tokens. It prints `uid <uid>: role <role>, player <n|none>. Sign out and back in to pick it up.` `--check` prints `key ok: project <projectId>`, then `index ok: players/player` when the published rules have the index (it runs the duplicate-number lookup's query; without the index it prints the fix and exits 1), and one line per account that has a role. Firebase's raw missing-index error (`Index not defined, add ".indexOn": ...`) never reaches the terminal: any run that meets it prints one line instead, "The Realtime Database has no index on players/player: paste game/firebase/database.rules.json into the Firebase console under Realtime Database > Rules and click Publish." (exit 1). `set_role.mjs` loads `game/.env.local` first, so `SBA_FIREBASE_ADMIN_KEY` can live there (the dev server reads the `SBA_` variables from the same file); a variable already in the environment wins. The database is `https://<project id>-default-rtdb.firebaseio.com` unless `SBA_FIREBASE_DATABASE_URL` says otherwise. With no key file it exits 2 naming the expected path and `SBA_FIREBASE_ADMIN_KEY`. The Realtime Database rules (`game/firebase/database.rules.json`: read and write false, plus an index on `players/player` for the duplicate-number lookup) deny everything to clients; the Admin SDK bypasses them, and FEAT-205 and FEAT-021 open paths when the game first reads them. The rules are pasted into the console by hand (Realtime Database > Rules > Publish): after changing the file, paste it again.

**Firebase console settings** (how the project `snowboardanywhere` is set up):
- Authentication > Sign-in method: GitHub, with the OAuth app's client id and secret; the GitHub OAuth app's authorization callback URL is the one the console shows (`https://snowboardanywhere.firebaseapp.com/__/auth/handler`). Email/Password (FEAT-274), with the email link option off. Until Email/Password is on, the panel's password buttons say "Email and password sign-in is not turned on for this game."
- Authentication > Settings > User account linking: "Link accounts that use the same email" (Firebase's one account per email address). A sign-in or sign-up whose email already belongs to another account is then refused (`auth/account-exists-with-different-credential`, `auth/email-already-in-use`) and never joined to it; `linkWithCredential` and `linkWithPopup` run only from the signed-in account panel (FEAT-274).
- Email enumeration protection: on (the default for a new project; see Email and password above). The verification email is Firebase's default template (Authentication > Templates).
- Authentication > Settings > Authorized domains: `localhost`, `snowboardanywhere.firebaseapp.com`, `snowboardanywhere.web.app`.
- Realtime Database (locked mode) at `https://snowboardanywhere-default-rtdb.firebaseio.com`, with the rules from `game/firebase/database.rules.json`. No Analytics.
- The service-account key is downloaded to `~/.config/snowboardanywhere/` and referenced from `game/.env.local`.
